### Networks (Communication Technologies)
- A network is a system where two or more devices are connected so they can communicate, share resources, and exchange data.
- Types of networks include: *Wi-Fi network, Cellular Network*

- #### Ad hoc Networks
	- They are temporary networks created without a central router or access point.
	- **Types of Ad hoc networks**
		- Open Wi-Fi
			- A Wi-Fi network without a password where devices connect directly to the access point
		- Tethering
			- Also known as a Personal Hotspot when a smartphone shares its mobile data connection with other devices via **Wi-Fi, Bluetooth or USB**
			- Devices connect directly to the smartphone instead of a router
	- **Performance issues with Ad hoc networks**
		- They often have limited performance compared to a traditional network, including:
			- Limited Range
				- They only cover a small range
			- Shared Bandwidth
				- Multiple devices using the same connection can slow speeds
			- Interference
				- Other wireless devices (such as microwaves and Bluetooth) can reduce performance
			- Device dependency
				- If the host device moves or loses power the network is disrupted

### Network Availability (Communication Technologies)
- #### Issues affecting Network Availability
	- Rural vs City locations
		- Rural areas may have fewer cell towers or broadband options, leading to poor connectivity and blackspots. Cities have denser infrastructure and better coverage.
	- Developed vs Developing Countries
		- Developed countries often have reliable broadband, 4G/5G coverage, and fibre networks. Developing countries may rely on older technology with lower speeds and less coverage.
	- Available Infrastructure
		- The number of towers, fibre connections, and network equipment affects reliability and speed.
	- Mobile Network Coverage
		- Some areas may lack 4G/5G coverage, limiting hotspot performance. Rural “blackspots” are common in valleys or remote areas.
